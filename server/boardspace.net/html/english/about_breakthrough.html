Breakthrough

Breakthrough was designed by Dan Troyka in 2000, and won the 2001 8x8 Game Design Competition.  It uses ordinary checkers equipment, and can be explained in a minute, but it plays with surprising depth.

Each player starts with two full rows of pieces on his own side of the board.  Pieces move one square straight forward or diagonally forward to an empty square.  Captures are made only diagonally forward, by moving onto the enemy piece.  Capturing is never required.

The first player to reach the opponent's home row wins.  A player who captures all the opponent's pieces also wins.  Since every move goes forward, draws are impossible.

Breakthrough is played on the standard 8x8 board, and also on the original 7x7 board.

Robots: the robot players search a few moves ahead.  They're good at spotting a breakthrough, but not much else.

Coding:  Breakthrough is also a breakthrough in game coding.  As an experiment, I pointed claude code at my implementation of chess, and at the rules of Breakthrough, and said "make it so".   This final result is still at least 90% that claude wrought.  You can read the actual prompts here.
The back story:  I learned breakthrough while playing in the annual Mochalunt abstract games tournament.  It's a winner!
